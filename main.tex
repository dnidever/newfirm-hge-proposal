\sciencejustification

\noindent\textbf{The hidden metal-poor Milky Way.---}
The oldest stars preserve a fossil record of the Milky Way's formation, but our
view of that record is strongly biased against the inner disk, bar, and bulge.
Extinction and crowding make these regions inaccessible to most optical
metal-poor-star searches, while conventional near-infrared broad-band colors
cannot cleanly separate metallicity from effective temperature and reddening.
Consequently, metal-poor stars at low Galactic latitude remain among the least
completely mapped components of the Galaxy. Finding these stars is essential
for determining whether the inner Galaxy contains the centrally concentrated
remnant of the proto-Milky Way, debris from its earliest accretion events, or a
metal-weak extension of the disk. Their spatial distribution, kinematics, and
chemical-abundance patterns directly test models for the assembly of the disk
and bulge.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{A photometric solution in the near infrared.---}
We propose an intermediate-band imaging survey of the Milky Way midplane with
NEWFIRM on the Blanco 4-m telescope. The NEWFIRM \need{final filter list:
J2/J3/H1/H2 or J1/J2/J3/H1/H2} filters sample cool giants across the $J$ and
$H$ windows. Synthetic photometry from stellar isochrones demonstrates that
the $H1-H2$ color is sensitive to metallicity along the red-giant branch while
complementary colors constrain temperature and reddening (Figure~1). The
observations will test and calibrate a selection designed to identify stars
with $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]<-1$ where optical metallicity surveys are ineffective.
At the required precision of \need{$\sigma(H1-H2)$}, our models predict
\need{completeness} completeness and \need{purity} purity over
\need{temperature/color and extinction range}.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Enabling the Hidden Galaxy Explorer.---}
After-SDSS-V's Hidden Galaxy Explorer (HGE) is being developed to map the
dust-obscured far side of the Milky Way with deep near-infrared spectroscopy of
faint luminous giants. HGE can deliver precise velocities and detailed
abundances for these stars, but its return depends on an efficient and
well-characterized input catalog. Existing 2MASS, VIRAC2, and APOGLIMPSE
photometry can identify luminous red giants and estimate extinction; it cannot
efficiently isolate the rare metal-poor population. NEWFIRM supplies the
missing metallicity information. Combining the proposed photometry with these
catalogs will yield \need{number} high-priority candidates to HGE's faint limit
of \need{$H$-band limit} across \need{survey area and longitude range}. Stars
in fields overlapping APOGEE will provide an immediate spectroscopic training
and validation sample.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Goals and legacy value.---}
The program has three measurable goals: (1) demonstrate and calibrate
intermediate-band selection of $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]<-1$ RGB stars in crowded,
extinguished midplane fields; (2) deliver a catalog with a quantified selection
function for HGE targeting; and (3) determine the surface density of metal-poor
giants as a function of Galactic position. The images and catalogs will also
provide a deep, uniform near-infrared data set for stellar populations and
Galactic structure in a region poorly served by existing surveys.

\expdesign

\noindent\textbf{Survey design.---}
We request \need{number of nights; dark/gray/bright} with Blanco/NEWFIRM to
image \need{area} of the southern Galactic midplane in \need{filter list}. The
footprint comprises \need{number} NEWFIRM tiles spanning \need{$l,b$ limits}.
It is chosen to \need{state optimization: inner-disk/bar sightlines, HGE fields,
APOGEE validation, and/or extinction range}. Each tile will use a
\need{number/geometry}-position dither that fills detector gaps, rejects bad
pixels and cosmic rays, and supports a spatially and temporally local sky frame.
Small offsets and \need{larger sky offsets, if required in crowded fields} will
be interleaved to control the variable near-infrared background.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Depth and exposure times.---}
Our quantitative requirement is $\sigma(H1-H2)<\need{value}$ at
$H=\need{HGE target limit}$, corresponding to signal-to-noise
$>\need{value}$ in each intermediate band. The NEWFIRM exposure-time
calculator gives individual exposures of \need{DIT and coadds} and total
integrations of \need{per-filter times} per tile under \need{image quality, sky
brightness, airmass, and water-vapor assumptions}. Individual integrations
will avoid saturation and background nonlinearity while providing efficient
sky sampling. Including \need{readout, dithering, filter changes, acquisition,
and calibration overhead fraction}, each tile requires \need{elapsed time}; the
full program requires \need{total hours/nights}.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Conditions and scheduling.---}
We require \need{requested image quality} image quality to limit blending in
the dense midplane and photometric conditions for stable colors. The program
can otherwise be executed in \need{lunar phase} time and has no cadence or
time-critical constraints. The fields are observable during \need{date/LST
range}; distributing them across the semester does not compromise the science.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Reduction, calibration, and validation.---}
Processing will remove detector signatures, construct sky frames from the
dithers, correct astrometry, and produce stacked images and PSF photometry for
crowded fields. Astrometry and broad-band zeropoints will be tied to
\need{2MASS/VIRAC2}; intermediate-band zeropoints and spatial color terms will
be determined from repeat observations and stellar-locus constraints
\need{plus standards, if applicable}. Cross-matching to 2MASS, VIRAC2,
APOGLIMPSE, Gaia, and APOGEE will provide extinction estimates, giant selection,
and spectroscopic validation. Artificial-star tests will quantify completeness.
The products will be calibrated images, a merged multi-band catalog, and an
HGE-ready target list with selection probabilities as functions of magnitude,
color, crowding, and position.
