\sciencejustification

{\bf Unveiling the Fossilized relics of the early Milky Way with NEWFIRM}

\noindent\textbf{The hidden metal-poor Milky Way.---}
The oldest stars preserve a fossil record of the Milky Way's formation, but our
view of that record is strongly biased against the inner disk, bar, and bulge.
Extinction and crowding make these regions inaccessible to most optical
metal-poor-star searches, while conventional near-infrared broad-band colors
cannot cleanly separate metallicity from effective temperature and reddening.
Consequently, metal-poor stars at low Galactic latitude remain among the least
completely mapped components of the Galaxy. Finding these stars is essential
for determining whether the inner Galaxy contains the centrally concentrated
remnant of the proto-Milky Way, debris from its earliest accretion events, or a
metal-weak extension of the disk. Their spatial distribution, kinematics, and
chemical-abundance patterns directly test models for the assembly of the disk
and bulge.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{A photometric solution in the near infrared.---}
We propose an intermediate-band imaging survey of the Milky Way midplane with
NEWFIRM on the Blanco 4-m telescope. The NEWFIRM $H1/H2$ filters sample cool giants across the $J$ and $H$ windows. Synthetic photometry from stellar isochrones demonstrates that
the $H1-H2$ color is sensitive to metallicity along the red-giant branch while
complementary colors constrain temperature and reddening (Figure~1). The
observations will test and calibrate a selection designed to identify stars
with $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]<-1$ where optical metallicity surveys are ineffective.
At the required precision of $\sigma(H1-H2)$=0.01 mag, our models predict
\need{completeness} completeness and \need{purity} purity over
\need{temperature/color and extinction range}.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Enabling the Hidden Galaxy Explorer.---}
After-SDSS-V's (AS-5) Hidden Galaxy Explorer (HGE) is being developed to map the
dust-obscured far side of the Milky Way with deep near-infrared spectroscopy of
faint luminous giants. HGE can deliver precise velocities and detailed
abundances for these stars, but its return depends on an efficient and
well-characterized input catalog. Existing 2MASS, VIRAC2, UKIDSS-GPS and GLIMPSE
photometry can identify luminous red giants and estimate extinction via RJCE (Majewski et al.\ 2011); it cannot
efficiently isolate the rare metal-poor population. NEWFIRM supplies the
missing metallicity information. Combining the proposed photometry with these
catalogs will yield \need{number} high-priority candidates to HGE's faint limit
of $H$=15.5 across \need{survey area and longitude range}. Stars
in fields overlapping APOGEE will provide an immediate spectroscopic training
and validation sample.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Goals and legacy value.---}
The program has three measurable goals: (1) demonstrate and calibrate
intermediate-band selection of $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]<-1$ RGB stars in crowded,
extinguished midplane fields; (2) deliver a catalog with a quantified selection
function for HGE Year 1 targeting; and (3) determine the surface density of metal-poor
giants as a function of Galactic position. The images and catalogs will also
provide a deep, uniform near-infrared data set for stellar populations and
Galactic structure in a region poorly served by existing surveys.

\noindent\textbf{Survey design.---}
We request 2-3 nights of bright time with Blanco/NEWFIRM to
image 60 square degrees of the northern Galactic midplane in $H1$ and $H2$. The
footprint comprises \need{number} NEWFIRM tiles spanning $20<l<40$\dgr and $-1.5<b<1.5$\deg.
It is chosen to 
\need{state optimization: inner-disk/bar sightlines, HGE fields,
APOGEE validation, and/or extinction range}. Each tile will use a
\need{number/geometry}-position dither that fills detector gaps, rejects bad
pixels and cosmic rays, and supports a spatially and temporally local sky frame.
Small offsets and \need{larger sky offsets, if required in crowded fields} will
be interleaved to control the variable near-infrared background.

\subsection*{Observing Strategy and Time Request}

The survey will use only the NEWFIRM $H1$ and $H2$ intermediate-band filters. 
The metallicity-sensitive $H1-H2$ color requires high-precision photometry 
for red giants near the HGE targeting limit.  The NEWFIRM exposure-time 
calculator predicts that, in $0.9\arcsec$ seeing, a star with $H=15.5$ 
reaches ${\rm S/N}\simeq140$ in each band with 60~s of total integration. 
This precision is sufficient to separate the metal-poor locus in $H1-H2$ 
when combined with existing broad-band near-infrared photometry.

At each map position (``tile''), we will obtain three dithered observations per filter, 
with each observation comprising $2\times10$~s internal coadds.  The 10~s 
coadd time keeps the $H$-band sky well within the detector's linear regime, 
while the 20~s observation time limits readout overhead.  The three 
positions therefore provide a total integration of $3\times2\times10~{\rm s}=60~{\rm s}$
per filter.  Three detector positions are the minimum that provide robust 
outlier rejection: if one measurement is affected by a bad pixel, 
persistence, or another detector artifact, the remaining two measurements 
can identify and reject it.  Relative to a two-position strategy with the 
same total integration, the third position requires only one additional 
small telescope offset.  The dithers will span approximately $60\arcsec$ 
with irregular offsets in both coordinates, moving sources across 
independent detector pixels and partially filling the approximately 
$35\arcsec$ gaps between the four arrays.

The survey will be executed with the NEWFIRM \texttt{DeepSparse} mapping 
strategy.  Adjacent map centers will be separated by approximately 
$1600\arcsec$ ($26.7\arcmin$), retaining roughly $1\arcmin$ of overlap for 
astrometric and photometric registration.  This spacing supplies
approximately 0.20~deg$^2$ of new area per map position.  The map will be 
divided into $3\times3$ blocks.  All nine positions in a block will first 
be observed in $H1$, followed by a single filter change and a reverse traversal 
of the same grid in $H2$.  This strategy amortizes the filter-wheel motion, 
which can require up to approximately 60~s, over nine map positions while 
keeping the two-filter observations within approximately 25~minutes.

Near-infrared sky subtraction will use the temporally nearest survey 
exposures in the same filter.  The NEWFIRM reduction software selects up to nine images with identical exposure parameters, scales 
each image to the instantaneous background of the science exposure, and 
constructs a sigma-clipped median sky image.  Each $3\times3$ block supplies 
27 exposures per filter, ensuring that nine nearby frames are available for 
every science image.  The large map offsets cause stars in neighboring 
pointings to fall at unrelated detector positions, improving their rejection 
from the sky model.  We will additionally apply expanded source masks before 
forming the sky images and fit a local residual background during PSF 
photometry.  Occasional observations of lower-density fields will be used 
to validate the sky subtraction and can provide independent sky frames if 
required in the most crowded or nebulous regions.

A nine-position block contains 18~minutes of on-source integration in $H1$ 
and $H2$.  Including detector readout, small dithers, sixteen inter-tile map 
motions, and one filter change, we estimate approximately 29~minutes per 
block.  At 0.20~deg$^2$ per tile, each block covers approximately 
1.8~deg$^2$, corresponding to a survey rate of approximately
3.7~deg$^2$~hr$^{-1}$ in both filters.  Covering 60~deg$^2$ requires 
34 blocks, or approximately 16.5~hr of scripted execution.  Allowing for 
initial acquisition, focus checks, calibration observations, block-edge 
inefficiencies, and occasional repeated exposures gives a total requirement 
of approximately 20~hr.  The requested two May--July nights provide 
approximately 21--22~hr of astronomical darkness at CTIO and are sufficient 
to complete the survey.

\subsection*{Observing Conditions}

The program does not require dark time.  Gray or bright time is acceptable 
because the observations are sky-background limited and use intermediate-band 
$H$ filters, although fields should remain at least $30^\circ$ from the Moon. 
Photometric or mostly clear conditions are preferred for accurate $H1-H2$ 
colors.  Overlapping map positions and the dense stellar population will 
provide strong relative photometric calibration across the survey.  We 
request delivered image quality of $\lesssim1.2\arcsec$, with 
$\lesssim1.0\arcsec$ preferred to limit crowding and maximize point-source 
sensitivity.  The short observations will be obtained without guiding, as 
recommended for efficient NEWFIRM broadband and intermediate-band imaging.
