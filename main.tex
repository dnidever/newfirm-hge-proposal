%{\bf Metal-Poor Fossils in the Galactic Midplane: A NEWFIRM Pathfinder for the Hidden Galaxy Explorer}

\noindent\textbf{The hidden metal-poor Milky Way.---}
The oldest stars preserve a fossil record of the Milky Way's formation, but our
view of that record is strongly biased against the inner disk, bar, and bulge.
Extinction and crowding make these regions inaccessible to most optical
metal-poor-star searches (e.g., Pristine, PIGS, MAGIC), while conventional near-infrared
broad-band colors
cannot cleanly separate metallicity from effective temperature and reddening.
Consequently, metal-poor stars at low Galactic latitude remain among the least
completely mapped components of the Galaxy. Finding these stars is essential
for determining whether the inner Galaxy contains the centrally concentrated
remnant of the proto-Milky Way, debris from its earliest accretion events, or a
metal-weak extension of the disk.
%Their spatial distribution, kinematics, and
%chemical-abundance patterns directly test models for the assembly of the disk
%and bulge.
Their distribution and chemical-abundance patterns can distinguish stars
formed in the massive building blocks of the proto-Milky Way from early
accretion debris and a metal-weak extension of the disk, directly testing
models for the formation of the inner Galaxy (Rix et al.\ 2022).

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{A photometric solution in the near infrared.---}
We propose a 60~deg$^2$ NEWFIRM pathfinder survey sampling representative variations in extinction, crowding, and stellar density across the inner Galactic midplane. 
Metallicity-sensitive CaHK photometry has efficiently identified metal-poor stars in relatively low-extinction, off-plane bulge fields (Arentsen et al.\ 2020), but cannot reach the dust-obscured inner-disk midplane targeted by HGE.
NEWFIRM's $H1$ and $H2$ filters offer a near-infrared alternative: synthetic photometry predicts that $H1-H2$, combined with existing broad-band $J-K_s$, can distinguish upper-RGB stars with $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]<-1$ from the much more numerous metal-rich red-clump and disk populations (Figures~1 and 3).
Our expected precision of $\sigma(H1-H2)\simeq0.011$~mag at $H=15.5$ will test this selection at the HGE magnitude limit.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Enabling the Hidden Galaxy Explorer.---}
After-SDSS-V's Hidden Galaxy Explorer (HGE; SDSS Collaboration 2026) is scheduled to begin observations in mid-2027, mapping the dust-obscured far side of the Milky Way through deep near-infrared spectroscopy of faint luminous giants. HGE will deliver precise velocities and detailed abundances, but its scientific return depends on an efficient, well-characterized input catalog. Existing 2MASS, VIRAC2, UKIDSS-GPS, and GLIMPSE photometry can identify luminous red giants and estimate extinction through the Rayleigh-Jeans Color Excess method (RJCE; Majewski et al.\ 2011), but cannot efficiently isolate the rare metal-poor population. NEWFIRM supplies the missing metallicity information.
The proposed region, $40^\circ<l<60^\circ$ and $|b|<1.5^\circ$, contains 926,174 HGE giant candidates in the relevant magnitude range. Scaling the metal-poor fractions measured among non-bulge APOGEE disk giants (1.4\% below $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]=-1$ and 0.14\% below $-2$) predicts approximately 13,000 metal-poor giants, including about 1,300 with $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]<-2$.
Combining the proposed $H1/H2$ imaging with existing catalogs will identify and rank these stars for use during early HGE operations. Existing APOGEE spectroscopy will anchor the color--metallicity relation at the bright end, while subsequent HGE spectroscopy will validate and refine the selection at fainter magnitudes.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Goals and legacy value.---}
The program has three measurable goals: (1) demonstrate and calibrate intermediate-band selection of $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]<-1$ RGB stars in crowded, extinguished midplane fields; (2) deliver a reproducible HGE target catalog with documented selection criteria; and (3) determine how selection performance varies with magnitude, extinction, and crowding. The results will establish the scientific return and technical requirements for extending NEWFIRM imaging across a larger fraction of the HGE footprint. Independently of that future expansion, the calibrated images and crowded-field photometric catalog will provide a uniform near-infrared legacy data set for Galactic structure studies.

\expdesign

\noindent\textbf{Survey design.---}
We request two May--July nights with Blanco/NEWFIRM to conduct a 60~deg$^2$ pathfinder survey of the inner Galactic midplane in the $H1$ and $H2$ intermediate bands.
The footprint contains 306 pointings on a $1600\arcsec$ ($26.7\arcmin$) grid,
providing about $1\arcmin$ overlap for astrometric and photometric registration.
At each pointing we will obtain three irregularly offset observations spanning
approximately $60\arcsec$. The three positions partially fill NEWFIRM's
$35\arcsec$ detector gaps, sample each star on independent pixels, and permit
rejection of bad pixels, persistence, and other detector artifacts.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Depth and exposure times.---}
Each observation will contain $2\times10$~s internal coadds, giving 60~s per
filter per pointing. The short coadds keep the $H$-band background within the
detector's linear regime. For $0.9\arcsec$ image quality, the NEWFIRM exposure
calculator predicts ${\rm S/N}\simeq140$ per band at $H=15.5$, equivalent to
$\sigma(H1-H2)\simeq0.011$~mag before calibration systematics. This precision
is sufficient to measure the metallicity-dependent $H1-H2$ displacement in
Figure~1.

Pointings will be executed in $3\times3$ blocks using a script similar to the old \texttt{DeepSparse} mapping mode. We will observe all nine pointings in $H1$, change filters once,
and traverse the grid in reverse in $H2$. A block covers 1.78~deg$^2$ and is
estimated to require 29 minutes, including readout, dithers, map motions, and
one filter change. The 34 blocks require 16.5 hours of scripted execution;
allowing for acquisition, focus checks, calibrations, and repeated exposures
gives a total requirement of 20 hours, which fits within two long winter
nights.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Conditions and scheduling.---}
We request image quality $\leq1.2\arcsec$ ($\leq1.0\arcsec$ preferred) to
limit blending. Photometric or mostly clear conditions are preferred, but the
dense stellar fields and overlapping tiles provide strong relative
calibration. The program does not require dark time; gray or bright time is
acceptable with a Moon separation of at least $30^\circ$. The fields are
favorably placed during May--July, and the short exposures will be unguided.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Reduction, calibration, and validation.---}
The CTIO NEWFIRM pipeline will perform dark subtraction, flat-fielding, and
bad-pixel correction. For each exposure, a scaled, source-masked sky will be
constructed from the nine nearest same-filter images; every block supplies 27
such images. We will derive astrometry and crowded-field PSF photometry from
the sky-subtracted stacks and use tile overlaps to determine relative
zeropoints. Cross-matches to 2MASS, UKIDSS-GPS, GLIMPSE, unWISE and APOGEE will
provide broad-band colors, extinction estimates (via the RJCE method), and spectroscopic validation (for brighter stars).
Repeat measurements within the dither sequence and in tile overlaps will quantify photometric uncertainties and spatial systematics as functions of magnitude and crowding.
%Artificial-star tests will quantify completeness and color uncertainties.
The final products will be calibrated images, a merged catalog, and an HGE target
list with a quantified selection function.

\noindent\textbf{Data release.---} We waive the proprietary period so that these pathfinder data can immediately support HGE target selection and broader community science. We will publicly release the calibrated photometric catalog and metal-poor candidate list following their validation.
